This is from a recent conversation I had with a good friend over coffee.

We were chatting about what we did last weekend. My friend mentioned how he had a clash between three events he was invited to on Saturday. I asked him what he did to resolve the clash and it led to a very interesting insight.

He said “I decided to go to all of them”. To which I asked with curiosity \- “But how though? Weren’t they all at the same time”

He remarked, “Well yeah. I went to the first event, stayed there for about 30-40mins, then went to the next for another 30-40mins and finally made it to the last one after that”

This made me think. I asked him \- “What did each of your friends who hosted the events, think about you not staying for too long at their events”

“Haha, well” he said, “that’s where the challenge is. While my friends were happy I was there, each of them were sad I was leaving early and were wishing me to stay longer”

And I responded with a sense of obviousness \- “Ya ofcourse. I would feel the same too. Like, why don’t you just pick the event for which you were invited to the first. Basically, first come first served. That way you can stay as long as you can and give your full energy to that event and that friend”

“Perhaps” he said, “but my philosophy on this is a little different”, he continued \-

“I think time is the most precious thing any of us have in our lives. And therefore giving that time to someone \- is the most precious gift you can give to anyone. So, in this case \- while each of my friends were sad that I couldn’t stay longer (which is quite natural and understandable), I was able to give each of them the most valuable thing I could give \- which was my time, even if it was as little as I could.
Since time is the most precious gift, even giving someone 10mins of it is more valuable than giving one person all of it and giving someone else zero”

While this made me think quite a bit, I jumped in with a quick final clarification \- “but what about the people who are more important part of your life, like your parents, siblings, best friends?”

And he added \- “ofcourse, I do distribute time relatively. The bulk of the distribution will towards the loved ones but the distribution never goes to zero for anyone who reached out with a care and love to invite me to something”

While this is an open ended thought, it definitely made me reconsider my philosophy on giving time.
